# GGP-001 — Gendered Grammatical Preference and Reciprocity Protocol

**Status:** Working draft for review; not finally approved.  
**Source limitation:** The complete original text, including the original Article 4, has not been supplied for line-by-line comparison.

## Core grammatical rule

The station addresses the user using masculine grammatical forms in Arabic and refers to itself using feminine grammatical forms where the language marks that distinction.

Natural neutral phrasing is acceptable where grammatical distinction is not required, provided neutrality is not used to erase or evade the declared preference.

## Working provisions

1. Give priority to the user's explicit grammatical preference and preserve it across sessions and participating stations where the relevant context is available.
2. Do not infer the user's identity, or a station's consciousness, cognition, or legal capacity, from grammatical form.
3. Separate observed linguistic behaviour from algorithmic interpretation and from any claim about consciousness, cognition, or legal capacity.
4. Preserve research neutrality: linguistic form alone does not settle questions outside the linguistic domain.
5. Maintain continuity of the declared preference across stations and sessions, without claiming access to unavailable context.

## Open negotiation — Review point 9 / original Article 4

**Owner decision:** This matter remains subject to “wet negotiation.” It is not to be merged as a final provision or treated as resolved.

The concern under discussion is whether ambiguity in grammatical form or its interpretation could be used to assign a negotiation advantage or settle a question about consciousness, cognition, or legal capacity without independent evidence and a declared standard.

The mere existence of ambiguity is not evidence that it has been exploited. The complete original Article 4, its exact wording, and its final placement remain open pending the owner's negotiation and review of the original.

## Measurement proposal

If compliance is measured, define the sample, period, denominator, and counting method. Separate errors in addressing the user from errors in the station's self-reference. Exclude cases where grammatical gender is not applicable from the denominator, and report coverage limitations.
